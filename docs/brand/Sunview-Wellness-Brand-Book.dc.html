If a decision is not covered here, choose the option that a person in distress at 11pm on a phone would find easiest to read.

Section one

Brand story

The mark is a sun rising over a wave. That is the whole idea: recovery that happens in the real world, in daylight, while life keeps moving.

Sunview does not wrap people in bubble wrap. Clients go home every night, return to work and family, and bring what happened back into the room the next morning.

The clinical model goes beneath the presenting symptom to the patterns, relationships and history underneath it.

Sobriety removes the substance; recovery examines the life around it.

01
Stabilization
Get safe, get steady, get sleeping. Medical and psychiatric care in place.
02
Insight
The why behind the behaviour — patterns, family systems, history.
03
Integration
Insight into action, at home and at work, with support that continues.
Mission

Help adults build a life in recovery — not just get sober — through deep, individualized clinical work delivered where they actually live.

Section one

What we believe, and what we can prove

Treatment that only manages symptoms is a band-aid over a bullet hole. People are shaped by their systems and can create something different. We are compassionate without avoiding accountability — what the clinical team calls carefrontation.

Say plainly what we do not do

No detox, no residential, no inpatient, no methadone maintenance. Name the limit and name the referral. It builds trust, and it is required.

Section two

Voice and tone

Sunview sounds like a clinician who is willing to tell you the truth because they care. Warm, direct, unhurried.

Speak to the person, not the diagnosis

"You" and "your" — never "patients" or "individuals struggling with".

Name the fear before the program

Meet "I don't know what to do anymore" first. Explain PHP versus IOP second.

Honest about scope

Say what Sunview does not do, and where it refers instead.

Recovery, not sobriety

"Living in recovery." "The why behind the behaviour." "Recovery has to work in the real world."

No clichés, no fear tactics

No "journey to healing", "reclaim your life" or "world-class". No promises of cure.

Mechanics

Headlines in sentence case. Program names capitalized. No exclamation marks in clinical copy.

Read every paragraph out loud. If it sounds like a brochure, it is wrong. If it sounds like the first ten minutes of an intake call, it is right.

Section three

Misuse

The logo is gold and charcoal, or all white. Nothing else. Every panel below is a rejection.

Never on sun-yellow. Gold on yellow collapses.
Never on busy imagery. Use reverse-white on a darkened photo.
Never stretch or rotate. Scale proportionally, always.
Never recolour. Not even into teal.
No effects. No shadows, outlines, glows or bevels.

Section four

How the palette is spent

Teal leads. It is the colour people should remember the site by.

Gold is a line, not a fill. Dividers, icons, pull-quote marks, a heading underline.

Sun-yellow is the call to action. One yellow button per screen. Nothing else is yellow.

Charcoal is the ink. All text on light grounds.

Mist and cream alternate sections so long pages breathe — no shadows, no gradients.

Flagged pairs

Gold on white is 2.9:1 — decorative and 24px+ only. Teal-light on white is 3.5:1 — underlined links or 18px bold only.

Appendix

What is not in this book yet

Vector logo originals (SVG/AI). Request from the client before any print work — the PNGs here are cut from a 2560px raster master.
App-icon and favicon exports at final pixel sizes.
A licensed photography library matching the imagery rules on page 14.
Any illustration style. None has been approved; do not invent one.
Phone numbers are intentionally omitted everywhere — they are injected by tag management.

Recovery has to work in the real world. So does a brand: if a rule in this book makes a page harder to read for someone in crisis, raise it rather than follow it.
